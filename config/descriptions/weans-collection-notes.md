I'd programme this as a small film festival disguised as an increasingly uncomfortable family gathering. It begins as a joke, becomes slightly more confrontational, and ends somewhere unexpectedly affectionate.

The title deliberately twists the original line from Limmy's Show. But I wouldn't make nine hours of divorce jokes. The interesting subject is how families create their own versions of reality.

The three blocks form one larger work, but each should also make sense independently. That lets us broadcast them across three successive evenings or as a complete nine-hour takeover.

I particularly like the contrast between a 1950s Scottish home movie, an American independent film, a Maureen Lipman advert, experimental cinema, the BBC's earliest observational family television and a man on YouTube explaining how to tie a tie.

None of those items shares a genre. The curator supplies the connection.
